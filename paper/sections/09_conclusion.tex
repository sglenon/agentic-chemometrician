\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented the Agentic Chemometrician, an MCP-based tool system for
spectroscopic machine learning that achieves reliability through typed
contracts, deterministic core logic, and human-in-the-loop approval gates.
The central design claim of this work is that trustworthy agentic scientific
analysis does not require a more capable language model---it requires an
architecture that makes errors detectable and routes consequential decisions
through human checkpoints.

The headline contribution at the system level is a leakage-detection
mechanism that automatically identifies replicate leakage and group-leakage
risk in spectroscopic cross-validation, and gates downstream model selection
on the resulting warnings.
Replicate leakage is a pervasive and quietly damaging failure mode in
small-$n$ spectroscopic datasets; automated detection with HITL escalation
addresses it without requiring the LLM to reason about group structure.

At the evaluation level, the Layer-1 deterministic harness establishes
that the tool system behaves correctly in isolation: leakage is detected,
hallucination guards fire on fabricated metrics, HITL gates are respected,
and fallback logic is correct---all with byte-reproducible outputs at seed
42 across four real spectroscopic scenarios.

The primary direction for future work is Layer-2 evaluation: running a
live language model agent against the MCP server, recording its tool calls
and responses, and scoring the end-to-end behavior against the same
criteria the Layer-1 harness measures deterministically.
Layer-2 will determine whether the structural guarantees of the contract
layer are sufficient to guide a real LLM agent to correct analysis, and
it will generate the numeric results that are currently reserved as
\texttt{\textbackslash TODO} placeholders in this paper.

Broader future directions include expanding the modality registry to cover
UV--Vis and process mass spectrometry, automating hallucination probe
generation, formalizing expert baselines with domain-expert participation,
and integrating the method-memory tools with laboratory information
management systems to accumulate institutional knowledge across product lines.
